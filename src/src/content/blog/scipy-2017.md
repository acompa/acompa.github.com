Last week, I had the pleasure of speaking at SciPy 2017 in Austin about my work on modeling trends in music. Thanks to the SciPy committee and Enthought for hosting a wonderful conference with excellent speakers, and to everyone who postponed happy hour _just_ a bit longer to join me and ask some great questions.

Here's my talk. I hope you enjoy the memes and bad puns as much as I did!

<iframe width="560" height="315" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/ifcA3xK8wsQ" frameborder="0" allowfullscreen=""></iframe>

I especially enjoyed these other talks from the conference:

-   Andrew Therriault's discussion of [sustainable machine learning models](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EkDsOJlpMAw);
-   Jake VanderPlas's candid and thorough comparison of [distributed systems used for image analysis](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sQLmvBi8tH8&list=PLYx7XA2nY5GfdAFycPLBdUDOUtdQIVoMf&index=21);
-   Jim Crist's extremely useful advice on [debugging and profiling Dask](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mbfsog3e5DA&list=PLYx7XA2nY5GfdAFycPLBdUDOUtdQIVoMf&index=19);
-   Gaël Varoquaux's thoughts on [scientific software development](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eVDDL6tgsv8&list=PLYx7XA2nY5GfdAFycPLBdUDOUtdQIVoMf&index=16) (worth a listen, since he's an expert on the subject); and
-   Peter Skipper's [introduction to propensity matching](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h9mD43crH0I&index=36&list=PLYx7XA2nY5GfdAFycPLBdUDOUtdQIVoMf) as a tool for observational studies.

[Like Andrew](https://medium.com/@andrew.therriault/im-waiting-out-a-thunderstorm-on-my-way-back-from-austin-where-i-just-wrapped-up-my-first-scipy-67454f93f734), I'm also very excited to check out Andy Mueller and Alexandre Gram's [tutorial on scikit-learn](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2kT6QOVSgSg&list=PLYx7XA2nY5GfdAFycPLBdUDOUtdQIVoMf&index=3). The comprehensiveness of the library's documentation allows it to double as a text for an introductory machine learning course, and the only possible improvement at this point is a detailed walkthrough of those docs by two core committers.

I've also bookmarked [Jim Crist's tutorial on Dask](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mbfsog3e5DA&index=19&list=PLYx7XA2nY5GfdAFycPLBdUDOUtdQIVoMf). The Python community seems to be consolidating around Dask as the "native Python" solution for distributed work. While I only have experience working on HDFS-based teams, it's always important to understand other solutions.

In addition to these quality, hand-curated video selections, you can find the rest of the talks [here](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLYx7XA2nY5GfdAFycPLBdUDOUtdQIVoMf).
